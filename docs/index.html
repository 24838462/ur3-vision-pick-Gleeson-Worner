Part A • Modelling

Modelling, Controllability & Observability

A nonlinear MuJoCo robot model supports a nominal acceleration-input model for observer and controller design.

Physical Model and Nominal Approximation

We integrated the UR3, Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, mounted camera, contact geometry and movable cubes in MuJoCo. The arm has six directly actuated joints.

The mechanical model has coupled dynamics:

$$\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q})\ddot{\mathbf{q}} +\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{q},\dot{\mathbf{q}}) = \boldsymbol{\tau} +\boldsymbol{\tau}_{passive} +\boldsymbol{\tau}_{external}$$

Computed-torque compensation uses the model to approximate six independent double integrators. This approximation assumes sufficiently accurate dynamics and excludes uncompensated contact loads and saturation.

$$\mathbf{x}= \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{q}\\ \dot{\mathbf{q}} \end{bmatrix} \in\mathbb{R}^{12}, \qquad \mathbf{u}=\ddot{\mathbf{q}}_{cmd} \in\mathbb{R}^{6}$$ $$\dot{\mathbf{x}}= \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0}_6 & \mathbf{I}_6\\ \mathbf{0}_6 & \mathbf{0}_6 \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{x} + \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0}_6\\ \mathbf{I}_6 \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{u}$$ $$\mathbf{y}= \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_6 & \mathbf{0}_6 \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{C}}\mathbf{x} +\mathbf{v}, \qquad\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{0}$$

Discrete Model and Rank Checks

Our nominal controller interval is $T_s=0.010\,\mathrm{s}$, or $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Exact zero-order-hold discretisation gives:

$$\mathbf{A}_d= \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_6 & T_s\mathbf{I}_6\\ \mathbf{0}_6 & \mathbf{I}_6 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{B}_d= \begin{bmatrix} \frac12T_s^2\mathbf{I}_6\\ T_s\mathbf{I}_6 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\mathbf{B}_d= \begin{bmatrix} 5\times10^{-5}\mathbf{I}_6\\ 0.01\mathbf{I}_6 \end{bmatrix}$$

Scope and limitations

Simulation Assumptions & Limitations

Estimated States and Ground Truth

Our arm feedback uses Kalman-filter estimates derived from noisy joint encoders. Estimated angles also drive wrist-camera forward kinematics, PBVS geometry and operational settling checks.

Cube localisation, size ordering and pickup, transport-clearance and placement verification use RGB-D measurements. Spawn coordinates construct the scene; they are not supplied as task targets.

We record simulator ground truth separately for evaluation. Computed-torque compensation still assumes ideal knowledge of the gripper's internal joint states, while replacing arm states with observer estimates.

Perception and Dynamics Assumptions

  • Camera intrinsics and rigid mounting transforms are known from the model. The overhead camera and robot base are fixed.
  • Tabletop height is known. Cube measurement assumes upright cubes with sufficiently visible, approximately horizontal top faces.
  • Colour segmentation assumes one distinguishable cube per configured colour. Accepted cube sizes are bounded by the detector's configured range of approximately 15–75 mm.
  • Depth is rendered without added depth noise. Occlusion and rejected surface fits can still interrupt measurements.
  • Robot inertial and passive-force parameters are treated as known. Contact loads, payload changes and torque clipping limit the accuracy of nominal compensation.
Part B • Sensing

Sensing, Calibration & Frame Transformations

RGB-D back-projection, estimated camera pose and stable top-face measurements provide task targets.

From Depth Pixels to Robot Coordinates

The wrist camera renders RGB and metric depth at $320\times240$ pixels. For the ideal simulated pinhole camera, we calculate focal length from its vertical field of view and image height.

$$f_x=f_y= \frac{H}{2\tan(\mathrm{fovy}/2)}, \qquad c_x=\frac{W-1}{2}, \quad c_y=\frac{H-1}{2}$$ $$\mathbf{p}_{opt}= \begin{bmatrix} (u-c_x)z/f_x\\ (v-c_y)z/f_y\\ z \end{bmatrix}$$

Optical coordinates use X right, Y down and Z forward. MuJoCo camera coordinates use X right, Y up and Z backward, requiring an explicit axis conversion.

$$\mathbf{R}_{cam,opt} =\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)$$ $$\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{p}_{base}\\1\end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{T}_{base,tool}(\hat{\mathbf{q}}) \mathbf{T}_{tool,cam} \mathbf{T}_{cam,opt} \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{p}_{opt}\\1\end{bmatrix}$$

CameraFrames calculates geometry in a separate MuJoCo data object using estimated joint angles. The overhead camera uses its known fixed extrinsic transform.

Detection, Size Measurement and Stability

  1. Segment colours: HSV masks identify red, green, blue and purple regions. Morphological opening removes small components; erosion reduces mixed object/background depth at boundaries.
  2. Recover a point cloud: Valid depth pixels are back-projected and transformed into the robot base frame.
  3. Isolate a top face: A slice near the 90th percentile of base-frame height is fitted using SVD. The normal must be within 10 degrees of vertical.
  4. Measure the square: A fitted rectangle estimates top-face centre, side length and yaw. Cube height is inferred from the cube-shape assumption.
  5. Require stable measurements: Cube records require 10 accepted consecutive frames, centre deviations and size spread within 2 mm, and symmetry-aware yaw deviations within 5 degrees.

Measured sizes determine the largest-to-smallest stacking order. Colour identifies each object; it does not determine its measured size.

$$\Delta\theta= \left(\theta-\theta_{ref}+\frac{\pi}{4}\right) \bmod\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}$$

Fourfold square symmetry avoids unnecessary 90-degree wrist rotations. The purple marker has a configured expected width; support-face checks also use the size previously measured from vision.

Visual Verification of Task Progress

After lifting, after transport and after release, the task requests fresh cube measurements. We check the overhead image first, followed by the wrist image if needed. A top-face measurement is converted to an estimated cube centre using the previously measured side length.

Verification requires five recent visual samples with a maximum deviation of 5 mm from their median. Missing or rejected observations do not count as success. The task stops if verification times out.

Lift and post-transport checks confirm clearance at those checkpoints; they are not continuous slip detection. Placement is accepted within the current configured tolerances of 25 mm horizontally and 20 mm vertically. Observed errors from identified trials are reported separately below.

Part C • Estimation

Kalman-Filter Joint State Estimation

Six two-state filters estimate arm position and velocity from noisy joint-position measurements and nominal commanded acceleration.

Measurement Noise and Prediction Model

Each encoder measurement is perturbed by independent zero-mean Gaussian noise. Our final configuration uses a standard deviation of $0.20^\circ$.

$$y_k=q_k+\nu_k, \qquad \nu_k\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R)$$ $$R= \left(0.20\frac{\pi}{180}\right)^2 \approx1.218\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad}^2$$

For each joint, $\mathbf{x}_k=[q_k,\dot q_k]^T$. The filter predicts using the nominal acceleration command from the preceding interval.

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} = \mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1|k-1} +\mathbf{B}u_{k-1}$$ $$\mathbf{A}= \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \Delta t\\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{B}= \begin{bmatrix} \frac12\Delta t^2\\ \Delta t \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\mathbf{Q}=\sigma_a^2\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^T, \qquad\sigma_a=2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$$

Process uncertainty represents differences between commanded and realised acceleration, including uncompensated loads. The supplied acceleration is a model input, not a ground-truth acceleration sensor.

Correction, Covariance and Evaluation

$$r_k=y_k-\mathbf{C}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}, \qquad\mathbf{C}=[1\quad0]$$ $$S_k= \mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}\mathbf{C}^T+R, \qquad \mathbf{K}_k= \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}\mathbf{C}^TS_k^{-1}$$ $$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} =\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}+\mathbf{K}_kr_k$$ $$\mathbf{P}_{k|k} = (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{K}_k\mathbf{C}) \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{K}_k\mathbf{C})^T +\mathbf{K}_kR\mathbf{K}_k^T$$

The Joseph covariance update improves numerical robustness. Estimates feed the arm controller and wrist-camera kinematics. Simulator position and velocity are used separately to calculate errors.

Statistical Consistency and Tuning Trade-Off

The observer logs covariance, estimation errors and normalised innovation squared: $\mathrm{NIS}_k=r_k^2/S_k$. Under a correctly specified scalar Gaussian model, NIS has a theoretical mean of one. A mean near one alone does not establish statistical consistency; distribution, temporal correlation and uncertainty coverage must also be examined.

Increasing assumed measurement variance generally reduces reliance on individual encoder readings. Increasing process variance makes the filter more responsive to unmodelled acceleration. Both choices affect noise suppression and tracking responsiveness. We do not claim a precise phase-lag value without a dedicated timing experiment.

We compared 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20 degree encoder noise at seed 7 using the final implementation. Our setting changes injected noise and assumed measurement variance together, while retaining acceleration uncertainty at 2.0 rad/s². The results section reports this matched-noise comparison and its limits.

Part D • Control

Computed-Torque LQI and Visual Servoing

Estimated joint feedback regulates arm motion, while fresh RGB-D measurements correct task-space alignment.

Computed-Torque LQI Regulator

We augment the nominal 12-state model with six integrated position-error states. Discrete LQR design on this 18-state system gives LQI feedback.

$$\mathbf{e}_q= \mathbf{q}_{des}-\hat{\mathbf{q}}, \qquad \mathbf{e}_v= \dot{\mathbf{q}}_{des} -\hat{\dot{\mathbf{q}}}$$ $$\mathbf{z}_{k+1} =\mathbf{z}_k+T_s\mathbf{e}_{q,k}$$ $$\ddot{\mathbf{q}}_{cmd} = \ddot{\mathbf{q}}_{des} +\mathbf{K}_q\mathbf{e}_q +\mathbf{K}_v\mathbf{e}_v +\mathbf{K}_i\mathbf{z}$$ $$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{req} = \left[ \mathbf{M}(\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{full}) \ddot{\mathbf{q}}_{full,cmd} +\mathbf{h} -\boldsymbol{\tau}_{passive} \right]_{arm}$$

The dynamics calculation replaces arm states with observer estimates while retaining simulator states for the remaining degrees of freedom. Non-arm commanded accelerations are set to zero.

At a 10 ms sample interval, the identical nominal joint gains are approximately $K_q=29.3648$, $K_v=8.5409$ and $K_i=21.9352$. The design uses the error and input scales in joint_controller.py.

Acceleration is clipped to $\pm2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. Integral states are limited, and integration that would worsen acceleration saturation is blocked. Motor commands are separately clipped to actuator control limits.

Acceleration anti-windup does not explicitly compensate subsequent motor-torque clipping. We therefore evaluate saturation and tracking in the full simulation.

Nominal Stability and Practical Limits

$$\mathbf{A}_{cl} =\mathbf{A}_{aug}-\mathbf{B}_{aug}\mathbf{K}$$ $$\max_i|\lambda_i(\mathbf{A}_{cl})| \approx0.989901\lt1$$

The pole calculation establishes asymptotic stability of the unsaturated nominal discrete LQI model for a fixed reference.

It does not prove global stability of the full robot, observer-controller combination, contact transitions, safety switching or moving trajectory tracking. Those behaviours require simulation experiments.

Integral action reduces persistent position error caused by imperfect compensation. Observation and task settling use estimated position error and a rolling RMS velocity check, rather than demanding every noisy instantaneous velocity sample remain below a threshold.

We monitor joint tracking divergence and non-finite states. Recovery events remain part of the trial history rather than being treated as uninterrupted nominal operation.

Camera-Frame Position-Based Visual Servoing

PBVS uses fresh RGB-D target position and yaw. The known camera-to-pinch transform converts a desired grasp or placement pose into a desired camera pose. Estimated forward kinematics provide the camera Jacobian and current pose.

Let $\mathbf{t}_c,\mathbf{R}_c$ describe the observed target in the current optical frame, and $\mathbf{t}_d,\mathbf{R}_d$ describe the target in the desired camera frame. Our implementation forms:

$$\mathbf{e}_t=\mathbf{t}_c-\mathbf{t}_d, \qquad \mathbf{e}_R= \operatorname{rotvec} \left(\mathbf{R}_c\mathbf{R}_d^T\right)$$ $$\boldsymbol{\omega}=g\mathbf{e}_R, \qquad \mathbf{v}=g\mathbf{e}_t +\mathbf{t}_c\times\boldsymbol{\omega}$$ $$\dot{\mathbf{q}} = \mathbf{J}_w^T \left( \mathbf{J}_w\mathbf{J}_w^T +\lambda^2\mathbf{I} \right)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\xi}_w$$

Angular rows of the optical-frame Jacobian and command are weighted by 0.15. Damping is fixed at $\lambda=0.005$. Camera linear speed, angular speed and joint rates have phase-specific limits.

Phase Gain Linear speed limit Angular speed limit Joint-rate limit
Pickup approach 0.8 0.025 m/s 0.15 rad/s 0.35 rad/s
Pickup descent 0.8 0.015 m/s 0.35 rad/s 0.60 rad/s
Placement transport and alignment 0.5 0.020 m/s 0.15 rad/s 0.25 rad/s

Missing or rejected measurements produce zero PBVS command for that update. Persistent loss can pause the phase or invoke task recovery handling. Lower gains and speed limits reduce commanded motion near objects, but do not guarantee an overshoot-free response.

Part E • Integration

Reactive Safety Supervisor

Camera-based intrusion detection interrupts the task, tracks an obstacle when empty and holds position when carrying a cube.

1. Empty-Gripper Tracking

An overhead RGB-D camera detects the configured cyan intruder and estimates its motion with a position/velocity Kalman filter.

When the gripper is empty, the stacking sequence pauses. Wrist-camera gaze and distance feedback track the object with a nominal 0.22 m standoff, bounded joint rates and joint-limit handling.

2. Proximity and Motion Limits

Configured safety distances are 0.22 m standoff, 0.16 m warning and 0.06 m critical proximity. The controller uses its configured camera and pinch-point geometry.

Critical proximity requests a stop response. Commands are limited or decelerated by the safety state machine. Finite camera sampling and actuator dynamics mean this is not an instantaneous physical stop.

3. Carry Hold and Resume

When the task indicates a cube is being carried, a confirmed intrusion latches an estimated joint pose. The controller maintains that pose and retains the gripper command.

Physics, perception and estimation continue. After the configured clearance period, the arm returns smoothly to the interrupted task reference and resumes the sequence.

Detection Integrity and Evaluation Boundaries

Cyan detection combines HSV limits, RGB channel ratios, morphological opening, component area and interior-thickness checks. These reject thin green/blue colour-boundary artifacts that previously caused false detections.

Operational safety arbitration uses camera tracking and confirmation states. The keyboard spawn toggle creates or removes the test obstacle and supplies evaluation labels; it no longer gates tracking.

Carrying status is inferred from task and gripper commands, rather than independently measured grasp force. The detector recognises a configured cyan test object, not arbitrary people or obstacles. This is a simulated safety demonstration, not a certified human-safety system.

We log tracking, carrying hold, recovery and resuming states. Tracking-divergence recovery remains part of the results rather than being omitted from the trial history.

Evaluation

Final Seed and Noise Results

We tested four scene seeds at our final 0.20° encoder-noise setting, then compared three noise levels using seed 7.

What We Tested

Our final test archive contains six runs, each with observer, PBVS and safety CSV logs. All six runs recorded convergence in all 12 PBVS phases: pickup approach, pickup descent, placement transport and placement approach for each of the three cubes.

We kept seed 7 fixed for the noise comparison and changed ENCODER_NOISE_DEG between 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20 degrees. This changes both the injected Gaussian encoder noise and the filter's assumed measurement variance. Acceleration uncertainty remained at 2.0 rad/s².

This tests matched-noise performance in one scene. It is not a full seed-by-noise experiment or a noise-mismatch test.

Noise Rejection and Reconstruction Error

Raw encoder position KF position KF velocity

Our filter reduced position RMSE at every tested noise level. Percentage reduction increased from 36.1% at 0.05° to 52.0% at 0.20°. However, absolute position error also increased, from 0.0320° to 0.0962°, and velocity RMSE increased from 0.0176 to 0.0267 rad/s. A larger percentage improvement therefore does not mean the high-noise estimate was more accurate.

All three seed-7 runs reached convergence in all 12 PBVS phases. These runs show successful servo convergence across the tested matched-noise settings. Broader conclusions require repetitions and additional scene/noise combinations.

How We Calculated the Metrics

To exclude unequal holding time after servo completion, we analysed observer samples from simulation time zero through the first logged convergence of the final placement-approach phase.

This includes search, reacquisition, trajectory segments and preceding servo phases, but excludes the final lowering, release and withdrawal. It is a consistently defined analysis window, not total task duration.

We recomputed raw encoder error, estimated position error and estimated velocity error directly from measurement, estimate and evaluation-truth columns. The recomputed errors agreed with the logged error columns.

RMSE pools equally sampled errors across all six joints. These pooled values do not represent the worst individual joint.

$$\mathrm{RMSE}= \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N}(\hat{x}_i-x_i)^2 }$$

Percentage reduction is 100 × (1 − KF position RMSE / raw encoder RMSE) . Position errors are converted from radians to degrees; velocity errors remain in rad/s. Statistics are rounded for display.

PBVS Convergence and Validity

We treated each contiguous target/phase block as one phase and selected its first row with converged=True. The table reports the largest terminal translation and rotation errors across those 12 rows. Their maxima may occur in different phases.

These are estimated camera-based convergence errors, not maximum approach errors or independent physical placement errors. Later converged rows can differ slightly as measurements continue to update. Selecting the first convergence gives a reproducible event definition.

Some measurements were marked invalid, principally during initial transport before the support target was visible. Those frames are retained in the logs and are not counted as converged measurements. All 72 recorded phase blocks eventually logged convergence.

Filter Consistency

For seed 7, mean NIS increased from 0.841 to 0.925 across the three noise settings. Position-error coverage within the logged ±2σ bounds ranged from 97.07% to 97.31%.

These coverage values exceed the approximately 95.45% expected for an ideal scalar Gaussian ±2σ interval, and mean NIS values are below one. Together they suggest somewhat conservative uncertainty over these pooled windows.

They do not establish perfect calibration. Temporal correlation, joint-specific behaviour and model mismatch must also be considered.

No-Obstacle Safety Check

Across all six runs, the safety logs contain 23,638 sampled frames with no active test obstacle. None recorded a confirmed detection, and all sampled safety states were normal.

We therefore observed no confirmed false detections or sampled safety interruptions in these baseline runs. This does not prove a zero false-alarm probability in other environments, and sampled states may not capture a transient event shorter than the logging interval.

Because no obstacle was spawned in this archive, these CSVs cannot measure positive detection delay, stopping response or carrying-hold drift. We report separate intrusion tests below.

Separate Intrusion and Placement Tests

In our earlier seed-7 interruption tests, the empty-gripper response logged NORMAL → TRACKING → RESUMING → NORMAL , and the carrying response logged NORMAL → HOLDING_SAFETY_STOP → RESUMING → NORMAL . We confirmed resumed stacking after removal.

One combined-interruption trial also recorded an 8° joint-tracking-divergence recovery. We retain that event in the trial description.

The earlier baseline console log explicitly confirmed all three placements and completion of the sequence. Its camera-derived XY/Z placement errors were blue: 1.5/0.1 mm; green: 2.7/0.3 mm; red: 0.6/0.4 mm. An earlier carrying-intrusion run confirmed three placements with corresponding errors of 0.5/1.6 mm, 1.0/0.3 mm and 1.2/0.2 mm.

Those placement measurements belong to those earlier identified trials, not the six new CSV runs. They share perception and calibration assumptions with the task and are not independent ground-truth accuracy measurements.

The new CSV schemas do not record explicit final placement or sequence-completion events, so we report phase convergence separately from final-stack success.

What We Learned

  • Position filtering: All six runs showed lower estimated position RMSE than raw encoder RMSE.
  • Scene variation: At 0.20°, position RMSE was similar across four seeds and all 48 PBVS phases converged.
  • Noise sensitivity: Higher noise increased absolute position and velocity error even though percentage position-error reduction improved.
  • Feedback and visibility: Coarse transport can continue until a support target is accepted, after which PBVS uses fresh measurements to converge.
  • False-positive regression: The six baseline runs recorded no confirmed intrusions without a spawned obstacle.

Limits and Next Improvements

  • One run per configuration demonstrates these outcomes rather than a statistical guarantee of reliability.
  • The noise sweep uses one scene and matched assumed/injected noise. Mismatched noise and broader combinations remain future tests.
  • Independent final placement accuracy requires an evaluation-only comparison against ground truth and explicit placement/completion events.
  • Numerical safety latency, stopping distance and hold drift require logs from positive intrusion trials.
  • Ideal gripper-state knowledge, known calibration, rendered depth and nominal-model stability assumptions remain as documented.

Running the Simulation and Understanding the Files

Simulation Launch

Use the project virtual environment, install the supplied requirements and run the simulation from the repository root. The compiled model models/ur3_complete.xml and its referenced mesh assets must be present. 

python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python .\main.py --seed 7

Press Q in the camera window to exit. Space or P toggles the test obstacle. WASD or arrow keys move it horizontally; E/C or Page Up/Page Down change its height.

The website is static HTML. Updating this page requires committing and pushing docs/index.html, followed by the GitHub Pages deployment. It does not require recompiling the MuJoCo model.
